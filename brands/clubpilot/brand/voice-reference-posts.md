## 01 | WHAT WE'RE SEEING

**Suggested graphic:** Members don't want more information. They want better answers.

Clubs already create a remarkable amount of useful information.

The challenge is that members usually need one small piece of it at one very specific moment.

What time does dinner start? What's the guest policy? Is the pool open Monday?

The interesting shift isn't creating more information. It's making the right answer easier to find at the moment someone needs it.

That feels like a pretty good design principle for club communication.

## 02 | THINGS WORTH THINKING ABOUT

**Suggested graphic:** Email? Text? App? Maybe that's the wrong question.

We spend a lot of time debating which communication channel is 'best.'

But members don't all behave the same way.

Some live in email. Some respond immediately to a text. Others check the club app every morning.

Maybe the better question is: How does each member want to hear from the club?

Curious what you're seeing at your club.

## 03 | WHAT WE'RE LEARNING

**Suggested graphic:** The interesting part of AI may be what happens after the answer.

Dining hours. Dress code. Event details. Club policies.

AI can increasingly answer routine questions quickly. That's useful.

But the part we find more interesting is what happens with the time staff gets back.

A conversation with a new member. A warmer welcome at the first tee. Five extra minutes helping someone plan an event.

The best use of AI in clubs may turn out to be surprisingly human.

## 04 | WHAT WE'RE SEEING

**Suggested graphic:** "What happened to the greens?"

If you've ever been around a club during aeration week, you know the question.

Members aren't necessarily asking because the club failed to communicate. They're asking because the question becomes relevant when they're standing on the green.

That's an interesting distinction.

Information has a shelf life. Answers have a moment.

Modern communication should probably account for both.

## 05 | START A CONVERSATION

**Suggested graphic:** What's the question your staff answers 20 times a week?

Every club seems to have one.

Pool hours? Dress code? Guest policy? When the range closes? What's happening Friday night?

We're curious: what routine member question does your team answer over and over again?

No wrong answers. We have a feeling the comments could create a pretty good list.

## 06 | WHAT WE'RE SEEING

**Suggested graphic:** Club operations evolved. Communication is having its moment.

Tee sheets changed. Dining reservations changed. Event registration changed. Accounting changed.

Club operations have quietly become remarkably sophisticated.

Member communication, meanwhile, is becoming its own discipline - part service, part marketing, part conversation, and increasingly part AI.

It feels like the next interesting chapter for the club industry.

## 07 | LIGHTER / CLUB LIFE

**Suggested graphic:** The most-read club communication may still be: "Cart Path Only."

Some messages have a natural way of getting everyone's attention. [emoji in original]

Others have to work a little harder.

That's part of what makes club communication interesting: urgency, relevance and timing can matter just as much as the words themselves.

What's the one message at your club that members always seem to notice?

## 08 | WHAT WE'RE LEARNING

**Suggested graphic:** Broadcast less. Converse more.

For years, club communication was mostly one direction: write the message, send the message, hope the message gets read.

What's changing is the expectation on the other side.

Members increasingly expect to reply, ask a question and get an answer.

That doesn't mean broadcasts disappear. It means communication is becoming more conversational.

We think that's a healthy shift.

## 09 | WHAT WE'RE BUILDING

**Suggested graphic:** We're building around a simple idea: communication should adapt to members.

Text when something is urgent. Email when the message needs more room. An app for club news. AI when a member simply needs an answer.

We're bringing those pieces together inside Club Pilot because we don't think the future belongs to one channel.

We think it belongs to member choice.

More to share soon.

## 10 | OCCASIONAL PROMOTION

**Suggested graphic:** Three minutes is easier than three paragraphs.

We've discovered that modern club communication is much easier to understand when you can actually see it happen.

So we built a short interactive Club Pilot tour.

A member asks a question. AI responds. A club sends a timely message. The conversation keeps moving.

It takes about three minutes - and that's usually when the idea clicks.

We'll drop the tour in the comments for anyone who's curious.

## How to use these examples (from the source document)

- Do not copy the structure mechanically. Copy the tone: useful, friendly, thoughtful and club-aware.
- Notice that several posts never mention Club Pilot.
- Notice that most posts have no sales CTA.
- Questions are used to invite the industry into the conversation.
- Product appears naturally when it supports the idea rather than becoming the entire idea.
- The objective is cumulative: useful post after useful post builds familiarity and trust.

The social feed should feel like an ongoing conversation about the future of club communication -
with Club Pilot helping lead the conversation.

## Travis's annotations (not Byron's; added Aug 26, 2026)

1. **Structural variety.** Posts 01, 03, 04, 06 and 08 run the same shape: observation, gentle
   reframe, soft landing on "that's interesting." Three of those in a feed reads thoughtful. Ten
   reads like a formula. The batch plan should mix in other forms: a sourced data point, a real
   quote once quotes clear the proof ladder, a short list, a single question with no setup.
2. **Territory 4 discipline.** Post 09 describes the coming platform. Under the dual-track rule
   that is `building` territory and must stay framed as direction, not available capability. Note
   its own hedges: "We're building," "More to share soon." Keep them.

A formatting note for reuse: these samples carry the source document's hyphen-as-dash and
apostrophe style and two uses of "actually" (posts 07's sibling lines and post 10). House rules in
BRAND.md (no em dashes, no "actually") still govern our copy; the samples set the tone, not the
punctuation. The tension is recorded in the open questions list rather than silently resolved.
